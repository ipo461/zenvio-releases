## 2. What Zenvio does, and what it doesn't

Zenvio is a free game clipper for Windows.

**What it does:**

- While you play, it keeps the last few minutes of your screen and sound in a short rolling recording on your PC. The
  sound is your PC's audio (which includes voice chat such as Discord) and your microphone, if the microphone is
  switched on. It is on unless you switch it off, during setup or later in Settings.
- When you press your clip key, it saves the last part of that recording as a video file (a "clip") in the folder you
  choose. Nothing is saved as a clip unless you press the key.
- It lets you watch, rename, star, shrink, export and delete your clips, and can delete your oldest un-starred clips
  when you reach the storage limit you set (10 GB unless you change it).

**What it doesn't do:**

- It never uploads your clips. There's no account, no cloud storage, no ads and no tracking.
- It never injects into, hooks or reads the memory of any game. It only uses Windows' normal screen and sound capture.
- It never records or logs what you type. It only notices when you press Zenvio's own keys.

Zenvio only goes online for two things: checking for updates (section 8) and sending a problem report when you choose
to (section 9).

## 7. Your PC and your settings

Zenvio needs a supported Windows PC (currently an NVIDIA graphics card). It uses some of your PC's processor, graphics
card and disk space while it records, and its rolling recording is written to your disk. You choose the clips folder,
the storage limit and whether Zenvio starts with Windows; you can change these any time in Settings.

When the storage limit is reached, Zenvio deletes your **oldest clips that aren't starred**, and tells you which one.
Star the clips you want to keep, and keep backups of clips that matter to you.

## 8. Updates

Zenvio checks for a newer version about every 6 hours, and when you open the Zenvio window. It never checks while it is
recording. It asks the public GitHub page above whether there is a new version and, if there is, downloads it in the
background. **Nothing is installed until you click Update**; Zenvio then restarts. Your clips and settings stay.

Each update is checked to make sure it downloaded correctly. GitHub sees your internet address when Zenvio checks, as
it would for any website; the [privacy policy](PRIVACY.md) explains more.

You can turn update checks off in **Settings** (the update switch). If you do, you won't get fixes, including security
fixes, unless you download a new version yourself.

Updates can add, change or remove features. If an update changes these terms in a way that matters, Zenvio will ask
you to agree again (section 15).

## 14. Stopping using Zenvio

You can stop using Zenvio at any time. There's no account to close.

**To uninstall:** Windows Settings → Apps → Installed apps → Zenvio → Uninstall. This removes the program and the
"start with Windows" entry.

**What stays on your PC after uninstalling** (so you don't lose anything by accident):

- **your clips**, in your clips folder (`Videos\Clips` in your user folder, unless you chose another one);
- **your settings**, your own sounds and your list of starred clips, in `%APPDATA%\Zenvio`;
- **Zenvio's logs, clip thumbnails, temporary files** and your list of sent problem reports, in
  `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Zenvio`; older versions may also have left `%APPDATA%\GameClipper`.

To remove everything, delete those folders yourself (type the path into File Explorer's address bar). If you've sent
problem reports and want them gone before the 90 days are up, delete them in Zenvio first (Report a problem → "Your
sent reports"), because the list you need to do that is in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Zenvio`.

**If we stop making Zenvio:** we may stop updates or switch off the problem-report server at any time. Your installed
copy keeps working as it is.
